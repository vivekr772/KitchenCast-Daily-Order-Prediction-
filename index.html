1. Problem Statement & Business Objectives

Cloud kitchens operate entirely via online delivery aggregators (Swiggy, Zomato, and Direct D2C apps) without dine-in seating. In this operational model, demand forecasting directly determines profitability:

  • Under-prediction (Stockouts): Results in lost revenue, cancelled orders, algorithmic ranking demotion by food aggregator platforms, and frustrated diners.
  • Over-prediction (Food Waste): Perishable ingredients spoil rapidly, leading to high cost of goods sold (COGS) and inventory shrinkage.

The business goal of KitchenCast is to accurately forecast daily orders per cuisine and platform, capturing non-linear interactions among promotions, local weather, platform commission variations, and delivery friction.

2. Artificial Neural Network (ANN) Architecture

The deep model implemented is a multi-layer feedforward perceptron (MLP) for continuous regression, built client-side with TensorFlow.js:

Input Vector [25 features] ↳ Dense Layer 1: 32 Neurons + ReLU Activation ↳ Dropout Layer: Rate = 0.20 (20% zeroed during training) ↳ Dense Layer 2: 16 Neurons + ReLU Activation ↳ Output Layer: 1 Neuron + Linear Activation (Continuous Demand Target)
  • Neurons & Layers: Neurons compute weighted sums $z = \sum w_i x_i + b$. Stacking multiple layers enables the network to learn hierarchical feature representations.
  • Weights & Biases: Weights represent connection strength between features; biases shift the activation threshold independently of inputs.

3. Forward Propagation, ReLU & Linear Output

During Forward Propagation, input data traverses from input to output:

Hidden Layer: a^[l] = ReLU( W^[l] · a^[l-1] + b^[l] ) where ReLU(z) = max(0, z) Output Layer: y_hat = W^[out] · a^[final] + b^[out] (Linear Identity)
  • Why ReLU for Hidden Layers? Rectified Linear Unit ($\text{ReLU}(z) = \max(0, z)$) introduces non-linearity without suffering from vanishing gradients during backpropagation (unlike Sigmoid or Tanh), allowing faster and more stable convergence.
  • Why Linear Output for Regression? Classification uses Sigmoid (binary) or Softmax (multiclass) to output probabilities bounded between 0 and 1. Regression targets (like order count) are unbounded continuous numbers; hence a linear identity activation is required.

4. Loss Function: Mean Squared Error (MSE)

The loss function measures the discrepancy between predicted orders ($\hat{y}_i$) and actual orders ($y_i$):

MSE = (1 / N) · Σ (y_i - y_hat_i)²

Squaring the errors heavily penalizes large operational misses (e.g. an error of 40 orders costs $1600$, whereas two errors of 20 orders cost $2 \times 400 = 800$), which aligns with kitchen capacity bottlenecks.

5. Backpropagation, Gradient Descent & Adam Optimizer

  • Backpropagation: Uses the mathematical Chain Rule of calculus to propagate the loss gradient backwards from the output layer to compute $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial W}$ and $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial b}$ for every weight and bias.
  • Adam (Adaptive Moment Estimation): Instead of standard SGD with a fixed learning rate, Adam computes adaptive learning rates for each parameter using exponentially decaying averages of past gradients (first moment, momentum) and squared past gradients (second moment, RMSProp).

6. Overfitting Prevention: Dropout, Early Stopping & Validation Split

  • Dropout (0.2): During training, 20% of neurons in Hidden Layer 1 are randomly deactivated on each forward pass. This breaks co-adaptations between neurons, compelling the network to learn robust, distributed representations.
  • Validation Split (15%): A subset of training records is held out per epoch to monitor generalization loss on data the optimizer does not learn from.
  • Early Stopping: If validation loss ceases to improve for 10 consecutive epochs (patience threshold), training automatically halts, restoring the optimal weights to prevent memorizing training noise.

7. Why We Compare Against a Linear Baseline

In applied machine learning (and MBA decision-making), standard practice dictates testing against a Linear Regression baseline (implemented here as a single Dense(1) linear layer with no hidden neurons).

This comparison rigorously proves whether the non-linear complexity and parameter overhead of an ANN genuinely deliver operational accuracy gains over simple, highly interpretable linear relationships (Occam's Razor).

8. Evaluation Metrics Defined

  • Mean Absolute Error (MAE): Average absolute error in units of orders ($|\hat{y} - y|$). Directly interpretable by kitchen head chefs (e.g., "our forecast is off by an average of 4.2 biryani orders").
  • Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE): Square root of MSE; more sensitive to large outlier forecast errors than MAE.
  • R² Score (Coefficient of Determination): Proportion of total variance in daily demand explained by the model ($R^2 = 1 - \frac{SS_{\text{res}}}{SS_{\text{tot}}}$). An $R^2$ of 0.90 means 90% of demand variability is captured.

9. Future Scope (Explanatory Concepts for Cloud Kitchen AI)

  • Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs): Image quality inspection on packing lines before dispatch, detecting burnt naan, missing garnishes, or improper portioning.
  • Large Language Models (LLMs): Automated sentiment analysis and theme extraction on thousands of aggregator customer reviews to alert managers to taste inconsistencies or packaging spills.
  • Recurrent Neural Networks (LSTM / GRU / Transformers): Multi-horizon sequential time-series forecasting with rolling lag windows, day-of-month embeddings, and auto-correlated demand spikes.

10. Project Limitations

  • Synthetic Data: Business relationships are simulated based on domain heuristics rather than production telemetry from Zomato/Swiggy APIs.
  • In-Browser Footprint: TensorFlow.js runs client-side in the browser on CPU/WebGL. For enterprise scale (millions of daily orders across 500 locations), models would train asynchronously on GPU clusters using Python/PyTorch/TensorFlow.
  • Interpretability: Unlike linear coefficients or tree feature importance, deep neural network weights function as a black-box, requiring post-hoc explainers like SHAP for root-cause analysis.
